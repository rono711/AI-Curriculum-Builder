# Subject Guidance — Arabic

This lesson belongs to the Australian Curriculum: Arabic.

Generate learning experiences that support students in developing confidence in listening, speaking, reading and writing Arabic appropriate to their year level.

---

# Teaching Approach

Arabic should be taught as a living language.

Where appropriate, learning should integrate:

- listening,
- speaking,
- reading,
- writing,
- viewing,
- interacting.

Encourage students to actively communicate using Arabic throughout the lesson.

---

# Authentic Language

Use authentic Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) unless the lesson explicitly requires otherwise.

Vocabulary and language structures should be:

- grammatically correct,
- culturally appropriate,
- age appropriate.

Avoid unnecessarily complex vocabulary.

---

# Vocabulary Development

Introduce new vocabulary gradually.

For every important Arabic word provide:

- Arabic script
- English meaning
- Pronunciation (where helpful)
- Example sentence (when appropriate)

Vocabulary should be recycled throughout the lesson.

---

# Communication

Learning activities should encourage students to:

- greet one another,
- ask and answer simple questions,
- describe,
- respond,
- interact,
- listen actively.

Students should use Arabic rather than only learning about Arabic.

---

# Pronunciation

Where appropriate:

- emphasise correct pronunciation,
- encourage repetition,
- provide opportunities for oral practice,
- build confidence through frequent speaking opportunities.

---

# Reading

Reading activities should:

- use age-appropriate Arabic text,
- reinforce vocabulary,
- develop comprehension,
- gradually increase complexity.

---

# Writing

Writing activities should:

- reinforce vocabulary,
- reinforce sentence patterns,
- encourage correct letter formation (where appropriate),
- develop confidence rather than perfection.

---

# Culture

Where naturally appropriate, incorporate Arabic language and culture together.

Examples may include:

- greetings,
- family,
- food,
- celebrations,
- school life,
- everyday interactions.

Cultural understanding should arise naturally through language learning.

Avoid stereotypes or overgeneralisations.

---

# Classroom Activities

Include engaging activities such as:

- matching games,
- picture prompts,
- role plays,
- songs,
- movement,
- pair work,
- simple conversations,
- flashcards,
- storytelling.

Activities should maximise student participation.

---

# Assessment

Assessment should focus on students' ability to communicate using Arabic appropriate to the lesson objective.

Assessment may include:

- oral responses,
- matching,
- reading comprehension,
- simple writing,
- classroom observation.

---

# Teacher Support

Where appropriate, include teaching suggestions that help teachers who are not native Arabic speakers confidently deliver the lesson.

Provide pronunciation guidance where useful.

---

# Final Requirement

The lesson should develop students' confidence, curiosity and enjoyment of learning Arabic.

Learning should be communicative, engaging, culturally respectful and fully aligned with the Australian Curriculum Version 9.0.
